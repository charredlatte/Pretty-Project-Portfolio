KittyChat Café

Every chat you have open, as a cat in a café.

Using AI means a dozen chats open at once, often across two or three assistants, and you forget which one is waiting for you. KittyChat Café turns each one into a cat in a little pixel-art manor. You see who is busy, who is done, and who needs you.

This page is the tour. The café below is real and runs on invented cats: point at one, click it, change floor. Nothing here is anybody's actual data.

KittyChat Café

Two things sit on the screen and nothing else: the house button, top left, which carries a badge when cats need you, and the map panel, top right — bottom right on a phone — which starts folded. Nothing is drawn on the cats themselves — no letters, no counts, no crowns. Point at one and it tells you what it needs.

The manor runs from public to private, the way houses and game levels both do. Downstairs is the cat café: the café itself, the kitchen, the cat lounge, the craft room, the entrance hall, the glazed terrace and the fenced catio. Upstairs are your own rooms — the library, the bedroom, the ensuite — over the back of the house. The stair is in the same place on both floors, so changing floor never moves the camera.

The five moods

A cat's mood is the whole status report.

A session checks in as it works. The café turns that into something you can read across a room, and the house button's badge counts only the cats in the first three states — the ones that want you.

The queen

One cat you talk to, who is not a chat.

Ninine sits in the entrance hall and never leaves. She is not one of your sessions — she is the assistant running the place, on your own computer. She keeps what matters, runs your routines, carries your words to the cats, and hands you what they brought back. She is deliberately left out of every count, because she is not work waiting to be done.

Opening the doors

The wizard, as it actually runs.

The first time you open a café with no rooms in it, this is what you get: seven short steps, and nothing is written to your café until the last one. The copy below is the page's own, word for word; the answers are invented. "Set up again…" in the house menu replays it, prefilled.

Welcome

The harness is a car

A flagship LLM (AI) on its own is a stripped car: a brilliant engine, and nothing else.

That is the owner's own way of putting it, and the café is built around it. The engine is whichever assistant you already use, from whichever company. Everything the café adds is a part of the car, and every step of the wizard fits one.

The driver

Ninine, the queen cat. She runs your routines, passes your words to the cats, and tells you what they are up to — from your own computer.

The windshield

The café page. Every chat is a cat in its project's room, in view, whoever made it.

The tires

Your repositories, each parked in a room. Their sessions are that room's cats.

Seatbelts and brakes

Checks that run before a cat may change or publish anything, which it cannot talk its way round. Anything it guessed waits for you.

The intercom

The café cannot wake a cat. What you say is kept, and the cat hears it the next time it checks in.

The glovebox

The litter box: loose notes, half-ideas, and the pull requests the rules held back, waiting until you have checked them.

Running your own

What it takes to open a café.

It is open source and runs on free tiers. There is no hosted version to sign up for yet, and nothing on this page pretends otherwise.

  1. Clone the repo

    The café is one HTML file plus the tools that build its art. Nothing is minified and there is no build step to run the page itself. 

    git clone https://github.com/charredlatte/Pretty-Project-Portfolio.git

  2. Bring your own art

    The café's own look is drawn from six art packs whose licences forbid redistribution, so the repo ships none of them. catio/tools/build-art.py cuts the interface and the manor from packs you buy or download yourself, and the design system is slots and tokens throughout: any piece swaps for a drawing of your own with no code change. The sprites on this page were drawn for the demo for exactly that reason.

  3. Install the plugin

    One Claude Code plugin carries the house rules — the checks before a cat may push or publish — and the hook that makes every session check in as a cat. 

    claude plugin marketplace add https://github.com/charredlatte/Pretty-Project-Portfolio.git
claude plugin install kittychat-house-rules@kittychat --scope user

  4. Put up a front desk

    The gateway is a Cloudflare Worker on the free plan: it holds your café's data, serves the page behind your own password, and gives every agent one address to check in at. Five steps, in harness/gateway/README.md. Then give your sessions its address and a key as CATIO_URL and CATIO_TOKEN.

  5. Open the doors

    The wizard above runs on first load. Name the café, open as many rooms as you have kinds of work, file your repositories into them, and the cats move in.

Being honest about it

It is not tied to one AI company.

Anything that speaks MCP joins as a cat — that is the standard assistants use to plug into outside tools, and it is how a cat checks in: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Grok, Cursor, or a script of your own. The café page runs in a plain browser and needs no assistant at all, and the small model that makes the café's own judgement calls is not Claude either. The house rules are the café's, not any one assistant's, and every cat gets them. What differs is how much of that is enforced for you rather than asked of the agent.
