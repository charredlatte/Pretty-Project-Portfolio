## Clock first: what a "frame" meant
NES, SNES, Game Boy and GBA ran at ~60 Hz (59.7 on GB/GBA); Super Mario 64 ran at 30 fps NTSC / 25 fps PAL. Every timing below is in the game's own frames; convert with the right clock.

## Sprite animation rates
Hardware refreshed at 60 fps but character animation updated at roughly 4–8 fps on NES/SNES (pixnote); 8–12 fps is the usual "retro feel" band, 4–6 fps for idles, 15–24 only for effects. Frame budgets were tiny: idle 2–4 drawings, walk 4–6, attack 4–8; a 4-frame walk (contact–down–passing–up) reads fine, and a 6-frame walk at 12 fps loops in 0.5 s. On the web: `animation-timing-function: steps(n)` with a duration of n/8 or n/12 s, no interpolation.

## "Press start" blink
Super Mario 64 (`src/game/area.c`): `if ((gGlobalTimer & 31) < 20)` draws PRESS START, so it is on 20 of every 32 frames: at 30 fps ≈ 0.67 s on, 0.4 s off, 1.07 s period, asymmetric and hard-cut. The same routine reuses the blink for "NO CONTROLLER" and over the attract demo. The PICO-8 idiom is the same shape: `frame & 32` (≈1 s cycle at 60 Hz, use 16 at 30 fps) or `n%16<8`. Sonic 1's flashing "PRESS START BUTTON" was meant to be there and vanished through a memory-clearing bug (TCRF). Keep it to 0.5–1 s cycles: WCAG counts anything faster than 3 flashes/s as a flash, and blinking that lasts over 5 s needs a pause/stop/hide mechanism (2.2.2).

## Cursor movement
The era's cursor is a tile written and erased, never tweened: Pokémon Red's `PlaceMenuCursor`/`EraseMenuCursor` overwrite one tile with the arrow or a space. Held d-pad has a key-repeat rule: `JoypadLowSensitivity` waits 30 frames (half a second) after a press, then re-fires every 5 frames (12 per second). RPG Maker still jumps the cursor instantly; sliding it is a plugin that adds a 1–30-frame slide with linear or ease-out. "Juice it or lose it" (2012) lists tweening/easing as something you *add* to a plain prototype, which is what these menus were. Config screens of the time (FF VI: Battle Speed, Message Speed, Cursor Memory) let the cursor remember the last choice.

## Menu open/close
Three families, all without easing: instant tile draw (Pokémon, FF, Chrono-style windows; the Pokémon dialogue box is drawn in one frame), a hardware trick (NES Zelda's screen-to-screen scroll is a deliberate PPU mid-frame write), and 3D showpieces (Secret of Mana's ring appears "with the single press of a button"; Ocarina of Time's pause menu wraps around the camera as a box). I found no citable frame count for A Link to the Past's menu slide or OoT's rotation; treat those as uncited.

## Screen transitions
- SNES mosaic: a hardware register ($2106) pixelates backgrounds; Super Mario World uses it for transitions lasting "only a few frames", and sprites are exempt (only tiles mosaic). Reproductions do pixelation then a colour fade.
- Circle/iris and shaped wipes: SM64's `play_transition` has FADE_FROM/INTO_COLOR, CIRCLE, STAR, MARIO (head) and BOWSER, with durations passed in frames: 16 (0x10) for the circle/star fade-in on spawn, 20 typical, 26, 30, 32 for the Mario head, 48 for the Bowser silhouette, 60 and 90 for long white/black fades; at 30 fps that is 0.5–3 s. Paper Mario also uses head outlines; SMB3 used a box shrinking to centre. SMW's course-clear iris-out has its own sound, "GOAL Wipe" (Koji Kondo).
- Game Boy: fading is rewriting one palette byte, so fades step through 4 shades. Pokémon Red picks one of 8 battle transitions by wild/trainer, dungeon or not, and whether the enemy is 3+ levels higher; circle types are preceded by three palette flashes at 2-frame steps; the spiral runs 120 one-frame steps (2 s); shrink uses 6-frame steps, split and stripes 3-frame steps.

## Typewriter text
Pokémon Red: per-letter delay is the low nibble of the options byte, Fast = 1 frame, Medium = 3, Slow = 5 (60/20/12 chars per second); holding A or B collapses it to 1 frame per letter; a scripted pause is 30 frames, skipped by A/B; "…" dots print at 10-frame intervals. EarthBound asks for text speed before anything else in a new game; FF VI has Message Speed (speedrunners use 1 with Battle Speed 6); GBA Fire Emblem offers Slow/Normal/Fast. Modern defaults sit in the same band: 0.05 s per character (20 cps), Godot Dialogue Manager 0.02 s per step with a 0.3 s pause at `. ? !`, UE tutorials 30–50 ms per character with 150–300 ms punctuation pauses. The skip rule: first press completes the line, second advances (Yarn Spinner's classic-RPG add-on speeds up and auto-skips to the next stop); Game Accessibility Guidelines make "progress through text prompts at their own pace" a Basic requirement.

## The advance arrow
Pokémon Red places a ▼ at the text box's bottom-right tile (18,16), toggles it on/off while waiting, then overwrites it with a blank and plays `SFX_PRESS_AB` when A or B is pressed. Its blink counters count passes of the wait loop, not frames, so the cadence depends on CPU speed: a web page should tie blink to a clock instead. The common form is a bouncing down arrow; many games swap in a second glyph (a square, in Yarn Spinner) when the speaker has no more to say.

## "Now loading"
Loading screens show "animated graphics, rotating symbols or other indicators", and progress bars are sometimes deliberately inaccurate. Namco put Galaxian/Rally-X on early PS1 loads (Ridge Racer, 1995) and patented loading mini-games (US 5,718,632, expired 27 Nov 2015); Resident Evil's door animations disguised loads; Crash Bandicoot streamed off CD with 3–4 s loads. WCAG exempts a loading animation shown alone from Pause/Stop/Hide, and MDN says keep loading indicators under reduced motion.

## Attract mode
SM64 starts its demo after 800 idle frames (no button, no stick) on PRESS START, about 27 s, cycling a demo list; Dr. Mario waits for its frame counter to roll over 8 times, 29–34 s; Sonic CD's title lasts 1530 (US) or 720 (JP) frames ×1.5. Nintendo's N64 caution asked that demos run day and night in store fixtures with counters that reset. Classic form: demo play, then high-score table, then title.

## Screen shake and hit-stop
Vlambeer's "Art of Screenshake" (2013) is 30 cumulative tweaks: screen shake (#15), "sleep" micro-pauses (#17, ~20 ms at impact), camera lerp and kick, knockback, permanence, more bass, bigger explosions. Era hit-stop in frames: SF2 ~14 frames regardless of strength (~10 in other accounts; modern games 9/11/13 for light/medium/heavy); Smash 64 ⌊d/3+5⌋ frames (15% → 10), Melee ⌊d/3+3⌋ capped at 20 (15% → 8), both parties freeze, electric ×1.5, and the frozen character vibrates (horizontal grounded, vertical airborne, per Sakurai). Rules of thumb: hit flash 50–100 ms (under 30 ms invisible, over 150 mushy), hit-stop 50–100 ms on heavy hits, shake small.

## Sound
Japanese UI audio has three canonical slots: カーソル移動 (cursor move), 決定 (decide/confirm), キャンセル (cancel). Pokémon plays one sound on text advance and none per letter. Modern guidance matches the old blips: navigation 100–300 ms and quietest, confirm larger with a rising interval (major third), error dissonant and falling (tritone/minor second), taps 80–150 ms, hovers 120–200 ms, mono, −18 to −14 LUFS, slight pitch variation to avoid fatigue. Transitions had their own stingers (GOAL Wipe); SM64 even voices "press start to play" on returning to the title. Accessibility: separate volume for effects/speech/music and never convey essential information by sound alone.

## prefers-reduced-motion, per element
`reduce` is not "none": keep feedback and loading indicators, replace motion with opacity/colour (MDN; WCAG 2.3.3 excludes colour/opacity changes from "motion animation"). Sprite walk cycles: small, non-translating, generally safe; stop looping idles or make them play on demand. Blink: fine under 3 Hz, but must stop within 5 s or be pausable. Cursor snap: no motion, nothing to do. Wipes/iris/zoom: full-screen wipes and zooms are named triggers; swap for a crossfade. Parallax: remove. Typewriter: auto-updating content, so offer instant-text (also the accessibility "own pace" rule). Screen shake: provide a disable toggle (Game Accessibility Guidelines, Xbox XAG 117); drop it under reduce. Hit-stop/flash: hit-stop is a freeze, safe; flash under 3/s and under 25% of a 10° field (≈341×256 px). Attract demo: looping motion that must be pausable; don't start it under reduce. Fades/slides under 250 ms are usually fine.

AVOID
- Don't ease or interpolate the cursor, sprite frames or menu pops: the era wrote tiles in one frame; sliding is a later 'juice' addition (RPG Maker plugin, Juice it or lose it).
- Don't time a blink or arrow by loop iterations or requestAnimationFrame counts: Pokémon Red's ▼ cadence depends on CPU speed; tie blinks to wall-clock (e.g. a 1.07 s SM64-style cycle, 20/32 on-off).
- Don't blink faster than 3 Hz (WCAG 2.3.1) and don't leave a blink running past 5 s without a way to pause, stop or hide it (WCAG 2.2.2); a press-start blink needs reduced-motion handling or a stop.
- Don't make typewriter text unskippable or fixed-speed: Pokémon, EarthBound, FF VI and GBA Fire Emblem all exposed text speed, and holding A/B collapsed Pokémon to 1 frame per letter; first press completes, second advances.
- Don't apply a mosaic/pixelation transition to the sprites if emulating SNES: the hardware mosaic only touched backgrounds and tile objects.
- Don't fire key-repeat immediately on a held direction: the era used an initial delay (30 frames) before repeating (every 5 frames).
- Don't run the attract demo early: cited games wait ~27–34 s (SM64 800 frames at 30 fps, Dr. Mario 29–34 s) and return to the title afterwards; don't start it at all under prefers-reduced-motion.
- Don't use a full-screen wipe, iris or zoom under prefers-reduced-motion; crossfade instead (Val Head; MDN) — but don't remove loading indicators or action feedback, which are essential.
- Don't make screen shake large or mandatory: provide a toggle/slider (Game Accessibility Guidelines, XAG 117) and drop it under reduced motion; keep hit flashes to 50–100 ms.
- Don't overextend hit-stop in scenes with more than two actors: Sakurai capped Smash's freeze because a frozen pair invites a third party; Melee caps at 20 frames.
- Don't convey state by sound alone or by motion alone; pair bleeps and shakes with text or colour (Game Accessibility Guidelines Basic; WCAG).
- Don't let UI sounds pile up: keep navigation 100–300 ms and quieter than confirm, confirm rising and error falling, mono, with slight variation to avoid fatigue; separate volume controls for effects vs music.
- Don't expect web audio to play bleeps before the first user gesture: browsers block autoplay, so a 'press start' sound must follow the press, not precede it (uncited, browser policy).
- Don't trust the frame numbers without the clock: NES/SNES/GB/GBA ≈ 60 Hz but Super Mario 64 is 30 fps NTSC / 25 PAL, so '16 frames' is 0.27 s on SNES and 0.53 s on N64.
- Don't cite A Link to the Past's menu slide or Ocarina's cube rotation with a frame count: no source for those timings was found in this research.
