## Who is reading (and why plain words are not optional)

- French TPE-PME: 26% used AI in 2025, up from 13% in 2024 (France Num, 11,021 firms). Bpifrance Le Lab: 72% of non-users "n'en trouvent pas l'usage" and over two-thirds of leaders cannot name a concrete use case. The #1 brake is "I don't see what it's for", not price or fear. So the page's job is to show one concrete, recognisable job done, not to explain the technology.
- US SMB owners (n=1,047, Q4 2025–Q1 2026): 86% rate "being able to speak to a human" important (66% very important); barriers are accuracy 36%, data security 34%, learning curve 31%. That is the brief for a done-for-you offer, a human contact in the footer, and a data line in the FAQ.
- WSU (>1,000 adults, 8 product categories): adding the words "artificial intelligence" to an otherwise identical description lowered emotional trust and purchase intention, more so for high-risk categories (financial services). Authors: "describe the features or benefits and avoid the AI buzzwords." For this audience the word "IA" is itself jargon with a measured cost.

## Section order, with the evidence for each

**1. Hero (headline, subhead, one CTA, product picture).** NN/g: visitors "often leave in 10–20 seconds"; the first 10 s is triage, so "clearly communicate your value proposition within 10 seconds". Headline = the outcome in the customer's words; NextAfter's "clarity trumps persuasion" test (literal "Get the Guide" vs value-prop headline) lifted conversion 44%. One CTA: Unbounce (18,639 pages) found 13.5% conversion with one link vs 11.9% with two and 10.5% with three; 1:1 attention ratio 13.8%. Picture: NN/g eyetracking shows users study information-carrying images (real product, real people: 10% more time on FreshBooks' staff portraits) and ignore decorative/stock ones ("jazzed-up = ignored"), so show the actual product screen or the real founder, not an illustration of "AI". Baymard: when the homepage fails to make clear what the thing is and why it is special, users "quickly lost interest".

**2. How it works, three steps.** Baymard: "users who are unable to thoroughly understand the services' core features will very likely drop it from consideration." NN/g on chunking: short-term memory is limited, "pack information into meaningful chunks"; the "magic 7" is a misreading, fewer is fine. Three numbered steps with one verb each, written as what *they* do (connect / ask / get), answers the Bpifrance "I can't see a use case" problem directly. No strong experimental evidence that three beats four; it is convention plus chunking.

**3. Core benefit as a story.** Van Laer et al. 2014 meta-analysis (132 effect sizes, 76 papers): narrative transportation shifts beliefs, attitudes and intentions; what drives it is an imaginable plot, identifiable characters, and verisimilitude. So: one named, believable owner (same trade as the reader), a Tuesday-night problem, one before/after. StoryBrand's SB7 is the practitioner version: customer is the hero, product is the guide, give a plan, call to action, show failure avoided and success. NN/g lower-literacy work (30% of web users): main point at the top, 6th-grade level on landing pages, "static text is easier to read" (no moving text under the story).

**4. Pricing: self-host / hosted / done-for-you.** How open-source SaaS actually present the free self-host route: n8n puts cloud tiers first and the Community edition under "Looking for something else? A standard, self-hosted version of n8n is available on GitHub", and, relevantly, labels one plan "Self-hosted only" with a link to **join a waitlist for cloud**: a shipped example of an unavailable hosted tier shown honestly. Plausible has a whole page, "Self-hosting is a real commitment", listing what you take on (installation, upgrades, uptime, backups, security), "community supported only", releases twice a year vs cloud "multiple times per week". Discourse keeps self-hosting out of the table and answers it in the FAQ ("What if none of these plans fit my budget? … 100% free and open source if you prefer to self-host"). Cal.com reduces it to a footer link; Ghost's pricing page omits it entirely. Pattern: hosted gets the columns; self-host gets an honest paragraph with the chores spelled out. For the hosted tier that does not exist yet: a card in the table, a real date or "not yet", price shown if set, one email field (waitlist benchmarks: email-only converts best; +1 field about −15%, 3+ fields −30–40%; cold traffic 1–3%, warm 8–15%). Do not take money. French law (Code conso L121-4, Omnibus transposed 28 May 2022) deems false urgency and fake countdowns deceptive in all circumstances; penalties can reach 10% of turnover. Done-for-you: real precedents are a one-time setup fee with a named person (Jetpack Workflow "Done For You" $1,500 with a success specialist; Noem.ai "White Glove" $2,000; eGrow setup $149). Price it as a visit, not a plan.

**5. FAQ.** NN/g: FAQs "still deliver great value" if they are real questions in the visitors' vocabulary, chunked by topic, scannable; never made-up questions. For this audience the questions are: where is my data, what happens if it is wrong, can I talk to someone, can I stop paying, do I need to install anything.

**6. Founder note.** 37signals uses the letter as the whole page (HEY opens "We need to talk about email"); structure: name the problem, why it matters, the fix, why you can be believed. Failory's caveats: works with a founder who has a voice, fails for commodity software and for readers who want brevity, so keep it short and after the facts, not instead of them. Landing Doctors: "We're early — here's exactly what's live today" plus a personal guarantee you will honour.

**7. Social proof with no customers.** Four honest kinds: founder proof (background), process proof (how it works, shown), data proof (numbers you can stand behind), borrowed proof (real integrations, real community/demo day, attributed research). Low counts backfire: "Join 47 users!" deters (negative social proof, Cialdini's petrified-forest signs tripled theft by showing many thieves). Since 28 May 2022 fake reviews are a deceptive practice in all circumstances in France, so a fabricated quote is a legal exposure, not just a style fault.

**8. Legal footer (France).** Four links, plus a human contact:
- *Mentions légales* (LCEN art. 6-III, now art. 1-1 after the law of 21 May 2024): société → dénomination, forme, capital, RCS/SIREN, siège, email + téléphone, directeur de la publication, TVA; EI → nom, prénom, adresse, "EI", RCS, email + téléphone, TVA; always the hébergeur's name, address and phone. 1 year + €75,000 (€375,000 for a company).
- *CGV*: mandatory B2C (Code conso L111-1, L221-5); B2B optional but must be sent on request (Code com. L441-1). Must state price, payment, how the service is delivered, rétractation, legal guarantees, the médiateur's name and site, applicable law; order button reads "commande avec obligation de paiement"; express acceptance (checkbox). Up to €15,000 / €75,000.
- *Confidentialité / cookies*: CNIL requires "Tout refuser" as easy as "Tout accepter" on the same screen, choice stored 6 months; audience measurement is consent-exempt only if strictly anonymous, no cross-site, cookie ≤13 months, data ≤25 months. Online cancellation button free and "directement et facilement accessible" since 1 June 2023 (€15,000).
- *Rétractation*: 14 days from the day after signing for services; exceptions when the service is fully performed with express consent, or digital content started with consent and an explicit waiver; not telling them extends the window up to 12 months; refund within 14 days; provide the formulaire type.
- *Médiateur de la consommation* (L612-1, L616-1): name and contact visible on the site, the CGV and order forms; €3,000 EI / €15,000 company.

## Copy rules

Unbounce 2024 (SaaS included): grade 5–7 copy converts 11.1%, 56% above grade 8–9; "professional" level 5.3%; difficult words correlate −24.3% with conversion, reading time −19.4%, word count −18.6%. NN/g: 79% scan; concise + scannable + objective = +124% usability; "marketese" costs attention; experts also prefer short (15–20-word sentences, half the words). Replace MCP/API/LLM/IA with the job ("il lit vos devis", "répond à vos mails la nuit"). One idea per paragraph, verbs first, numbers over adjectives.

## Playful / game-styled pages

- **PostHog**: the site is its #1 sales channel; brand rules are "slightly weird, not try-hard funny", "playful, not childish", "art accentuates content and doesn't distract from it", "lead with the specific, don't open with a mission statement", and they admit "ooh a hedgehog" is only interesting to insiders. The 2025 OS-style redesign drew huge attention and these reactions: "browser within a browser… disorienting", broken keyboard/scroll, mobile jank, "I bounced", "fun, but a disaster for conversions once launch hype fades". Right: personality in copy and art. Wrong: novelty in navigation.
- **Gather** (pixel-art B2B SaaS): the pixel look was chosen to be "fun and approachable"; 4M users, revenue quadrupled to $400k/month with Coca-Cola and Amazon. Critique: the aesthetic "stops fitting as teams grow… a real problem the first time a client or investor gets invited". Right: the game look lowered the fear of a new tool. Wrong: it reads unserious to outsiders.
- **Habitica**: works for its niche; reviewers note "the interface can feel overwhelming at first and pixel-art design may not appeal to everyone".
- **Lemon Squeezy / Tally / Basecamp**: mascot and doodles as accents on a plain, real-screenshot page; "simple reads as friendly, not basic".
- Measured gamified elements: a gamification banner +18% sales conversion (BRAME A/B); a 5-question quiz +34% subscriptions (Seamwork); a wheel-of-fortune page 41% conversion (Watery). All interactive *components* inside conventional pages.
- Jakob's Law: "users prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know"; deviate in look and tone, never in scroll, nav, pricing or checkout. Pixel fonts: WCAG 4.5:1 contrast, ≥16px body, 200% resize, rem units; keep body text in a real typeface.

AVOID
- Do not write the words IA/AI, MCP, API, LLM, agent or modèle in the hero, steps or pricing: the WSU study measured a drop in trust and purchase intent from the word 'artificial intelligence' alone, and Bpifrance shows the audience's blocker is not seeing a concrete use, not understanding the tech.
- Do not put more than one call to action in the hero; Unbounce's 18,639-page data shows conversion falling from 13.5% with one link to 10.5% with three.
- Do not use an illustration or stock image where the product screen or the real founder could go; NN/g eyetracking shows decorative images are ignored and real ones studied.
- Do not open with a mission statement or clever headline; a literal 'what you get' headline beat a value-prop headline by 44%, and visitors decide in the first 10 seconds.
- Do not invent testimonials, logos, review counts or press badges: since 28 May 2022 fake reviews are a deceptive commercial practice in France (L121-4), and fabricated proof is the fastest way to lose trust.
- Do not show small real counts either ('Rejoignez 12 utilisateurs'): low numbers are negative social proof and deter more than no number.
- Do not write 'Témoignages à venir' or 'Testimonials coming soon'; label the stage honestly instead ('Nous débutons, voici exactement ce qui marche aujourd'hui').
- Do not fake urgency around the unavailable hosted tier (countdowns, 'places limitées' without a real limit): deemed deceptive in all circumstances under Code conso L121-4; show the tier, say it is not open yet, take an email only, never a payment.
- Do not hide the self-host route or dress it up as effortless: Plausible's 'Self-hosting is a real commitment' list is the honest pattern; equally, do not give it a pricing column that competes with hosted.
- Do not ask for more than an email on the waitlist form: each extra field costs 15–40% of signups.
- Do not fabricate FAQ questions nobody asked, or write them in company vocabulary; NN/g: real questions, the visitors' words, chunked by topic.
- Do not let the founder note replace the facts or run long; Failory's caveat is that letters fail for commodity software and brevity-seeking readers.
- Do not write above a 6th–7th grade reading level on the landing page: grade 5–7 copy converts 11.1% vs 5.3% for 'professional' prose (Unbounce), and 30% of users read word by word (NN/g).
- Do not animate text or put the story over moving backgrounds: NN/g lower-literacy research finds static text easier to read.
- Do not make the game look structural (custom scrolling, OS-style windows, mystery navigation, pixel-font body text): PostHog's redesign was called 'disorienting', 'a disaster for conversions', and Jakob's Law says deviate in look and tone, never in how the page works.
- Do not set body text in a pixel font below 16px or under 4.5:1 contrast, or in fixed px that cannot resize to 200%.
- Do not ship a French footer without all four: mentions légales (with hébergeur and directeur de la publication), CGV (with the médiateur's name and site and the 'commande avec obligation de paiement' wording), confidentialité/cookies (refuse as easy as accept), and rétractation (14 days, the waiver wording for digital services, the formulaire type).
- Do not omit the médiateur de la consommation from the site, CGV and order flow: it is a separate obligation (L612-1, L616-1) with its own fine.
- Do not hide the human: 86% of small-business owners rate being able to talk to a person as important; put a real name and a way to reach them near the done-for-you offer and in the footer.
